# Idle management: return loops, monetization and fair competition

Research recommendation: keep the permanent business personal and playable offline. Monetize visible cosmetic value. Put competition in one optional weekly management challenge with identical starting resources and a fixed simulation budget.

This is a primary-source desk review, not hands-on play or an audit of these games' economies. Publisher support pages establish documented mechanics; several FAQs have older update dates. Storefront prices are US examples checked during this review, not a proposed price list or global prices. No provider configuration was changed.

## Recommended first TestFlight design — inference, not competitor facts

**Permanent business:** a readable 3D service chain, a small number of room types, staffing choices and visible patient/guest needs. Use one earned currency plus permanent reputation unlocks. A welcoming return report should show completed work and the next meaningful choice. Start balance experiments around 2–5 minute visits, a visible improvement within the opening minute and a generous 24-hour offline earnings cap. Those numbers are hypotheses to test, not proven retention targets. Do not reset a streak or damage the business because someone missed a day. Persistent character stories, collection pages and room transformations should carry the return loop.

**Weekly challenge:** identical layout, budget, available staff and arrival seed for everyone, independent of their permanent business. Give a fixed amount of simulated work, allow pausing, and rank the best completed plan. This rewards management skill rather than reflex speed or accumulated wealth. Let people replay without buying tickets; submitting more attempts should not directly add score. Show a clear score breakdown such as service completions and comfort within the fixed budget. Keep personal bests and the real Game Center rank; never seed invented competitors.

Use one seven-day recurring Game Center board, beginning at a stated UTC boundary, with new IDs after the concept is fixed. Weekly participation gives a casual player more opportunity than a daily deadline. Apple supports recurring boards and explicitly suggests weekly modes and seasonal events. Its native recurrence limit is 30 days: an initial season can be 28 days, but multi-month cumulative seasons need additional design/storage. Do not ship ranked leagues or percentile reward distribution without the supporting logic. [Apple configuration](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/configure-game-center/manage-leaderboards), [UTC properties](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/reference/game-center/leaderboards), [design guidance](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-center).

**Fairness boundary:** the main business works offline. Queue challenge scores against their exact occurrence and authenticated account; retry only within that occurrence and retain the local result when it expires. Never move a late score into the next week. A locally verified deterministic result plus sanity bounds is realistic for a casual TestFlight leaderboard, but it is not server-authoritative anti-cheat. Do not attach cash or valuable prizes. Competitive attempt enforcement, tamper-resistant clocks, paid reward distribution and stronger anti-cheat would require a trusted backend.

**Monetization:** ship one honest non-consumable decor/supporter pack plus existing optional tips. Use the existing verified Plus entitlement for equivalent cosmetic value so prior purchasers keep access. Sell the actual contents shown in a preview: room finishes, staff outfits, signage and ambience, with no speed/income/ranked bonus. Additional authored locations could become later content packs once there is enough content to justify them. Do not sell offline-cap extensions, queue slots, emergency relief or paid competitive power in the first build. Avoid subscriptions and random paid staff boxes for this scope. Apple's IAP rules support digital unlocks and tips, require restoration for restorable products and prohibit expiry of bought currency. [Apple purchase rules](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/#in-app-purchase).

**Ads:** the first candidate can monetize through real StoreKit purchases without adding an ad network. If rewarded ads are added later, use explicit opt-in rewards only in the personal business, with a clear dismissal path and no interruption ads. Keep them completely outside the weekly score economy. An ad SDK needs real account/placement setup, lifecycle and reward verification, plus a privacy review; a placeholder button that simply grants a reward is not an ad integration.

## Implementation boundary

Existing Unity rendering/UI infrastructure, saved-profile discipline, native StoreKit verification, restore/pending/revocation handling, Game Center dashboard and offline queue are reusable. Gameplay, economy persistence, offline catch-up and the weekly fixed-budget simulation need implementation and meaningful tests. Keep the old orchard boards untouched; do not relabel their scores as management scores. Update shop and TestFlight metadata only after the new concept and deliverable content are concrete.

A credible candidate requires actual Unity import/tests, simulator or device gameplay, background/resume and repeat offline collection checks, process-kill recovery, the real StoreKit sandbox and Game Center paths, a fresh device-SDK export, released-Xcode archive, and verified App Store Connect processing. Existing source checks or an old orchard export do not establish this new game's readiness.
